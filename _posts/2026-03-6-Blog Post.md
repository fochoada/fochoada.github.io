New reserach suggests how AI and Automation can improve digital forensics methods, potentially saving operational costs.

<h3>Costs Digital Forensics, AI, and Automation</h3>

*WIRES* Forensic Science Research team has uncovered key metric data of the impacts of automation and AI on digital forensics. Losses from Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3), FBI indicate losses have increased by 200% since the early 2000s.

[WIRES](https://gmuedu-my.sharepoint.com/:b:/g/personal/fochoada_gmu_edu/IQDTi8W_ByzkRaT0JUEIjXv9AVWHFrKP-rqaHFCLeolev3s?e=OUML51 "Link expiring September 2026") shares their findings from IC3 and other sources showing a total 3.5 billion USD in losses in 2019 alone. With high spikes of complaints and monetary loss in the early 2010s and in 2019.

To help lower costs, the research paper details how human-error prevention and automated systems reduce costs. Research sources include AI, automation, machine learning, and digital forensics studies. Findings include ways to reduce operational costs through error-prevention and automation tools.

Starting with AI, Jarrett from *WIRES* says, “one of the critical outcomes was developing a customized Decision Support System (DSS) leveraging the capabilities of AI and ASP to help resolve complex investigations that would be challenging if solved by human intelligence alone”.

Answer Set Programming (ASP) and DSS are used to analyze potential digital evidence. The goal of implanting advanced AI forensics is to create case-specific data. Integrating “both AI and automation techniques to offer measurable improvements to case proceedings,” says Jarrett.

Jarret defines automation in DFS (digital forensics) as the “use of computer systems to automate the traditional process of information acquisition, processing, and interpretation“. Examples being Self Organizing Maps (SOMs) and Automated Evidence Profiling (AEP). 
AEPs can sort through footage, files, or audio to find specific data automatically, while SOMs can take that data and sort it by likeliness. Guiding investigators to potential evidence. “Thereby making the entire process more efficient and less costly” (Jarret).

<h3>Implementation of Automation Technologies</h3>

The goals of automating DFS include cost cutting, human-error prevention, and shorter investigation time. Jarret explains how AI and automation can cut on time and money. Forensic imaging costs around “$5,000 and $15,000, while analyses and processing of more complex images and videos may exceed $100,000” (Jarrett).

Digital forensics, AI, and automation still have a long way to go. A constant changing landscape makes it difficult to train AI to find one specific form of evidence. Scattered and unstandardized evidence formats form inconsistencies in SOMs.

A solution proposed by Jarret is to standardize the industry, following government guidelines, and co-operation between tech companies.

“Encouraging information security experts and programming entrepreneurs to develop high-performing digital forensic tools through crowdfunding can help fulfill the pertinent gaps. Finally, integrating industry best practices regarding digital forensics and forensic investigations across the globe may offer useful information on optimizing the existing DFS services” Jarret claims.

There is a lot of potential for using automation in digital forensics. With promising results in cost reduction.
